PROMPTFROMZERO · DAY 82

Multi-persona prompting — at 60% shared context, five experts are worth fewer than two, and no number of them fixes a shared bias.

Five personas, and how many of them are really there

“You are a panel of five experts. Debate, then agree.” The pitch is five viewpoints for one call. But the personas share a prompt, a context window and a model — so their mistakes are correlated, and correlated voices do not average.

The floor you cannot get under by adding voices

Var(mean) = σ2(ρ + (1−ρ)/n). The second term shrinks with n. The first does not shrink at all.

And averaging is not a bias correction

If every persona is wrong in the same direction — which is exactly what a shared system prompt produces — no number of them helps.

Fifty personas, all two units high, give an answer two units high. The only thing that matters is the mean bias.

Which aggregation rule, and what it costs

The rule is usually left implicit — “then agree”. It matters most in the case worth planning for: one persona confidently wrong.

The panel, with correlation as an explicit knob

The sqrt weights are deliberate: they hold each persona's total variance fixed while share moves, so the comparison isolates correlation instead of confounding it with noise.
