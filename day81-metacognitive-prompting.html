Metacognitive Prompting — five stages that make a model check its own work, and the one stage that is worth less than it looks.

Answer, then argue with yourself, then answer again

Metacognitive Prompting borrows the shape of human self-monitoring. Instead of one shot at an answer, the model walks five stages: understand the question, form a preliminary answer, critique that answer, decide, and finally report confidence. The claim is that the critique stage catches the errors the first pass made.

To measure that claim rather than assert it, everything on this page runs against a deterministic model with known, deliberate failure modes and a task set whose answers are known. No API, no sampling, no luck — re-run it and you get the same numbers.

The stage that does not pay for itself

    

      Stage five asks the model how confident it is. That number is only worth
      having if it tracks whether the answer is actually right. Below, the tasks

The five stages, and what each one can and cannot see

    
1 · Comprehension

      Restate the question in the model's own terms. This is where a misread is
      caught — and only a misread. It has no access to the answer yet, so it
      can never fix an arithmetic slip.

    
2 · Preliminary judgment

      The first answer, formed from the comprehension and nothing else. This is
      what a single-pass prompt would have returned.

    
3 · Critical evaluation

      Look for a specific, nameable defect in stage 2. The word specific is
      load-bearing: a critique that produces no named defect must not be allowed to
      change anything.

    
4 · Final decision

      Revise only if stage 3 named a defect. Otherwise keep stage 2 verbatim.

    
5 · Confidence

      Report how sure the model is. The stage is cheap to add and easy to believe,
      which is exactly why it needs measuring.

  


  

    
Why stage 4 must be gated

    

      The tempting implementation lets stage 3 always produce a critique and stage
      4 always act on it. Every answer gets revised, and the entire gain evaporates:
      the pipeline lands back level with having run no stages at all. It is as
      likely to talk itself out of a correct answer as into one — and it even
      undoes the fixes stage 1 had already made.
    

    

    

  


  

    
Where the gains actually come from

    

      Broken out by the kind of defect each task was designed to trigger. The
      stages are not interchangeable: comprehension fixes misreads and nothing
      else, critique fixes rule slips and nothing else.
