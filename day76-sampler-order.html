PROMPTFROMZERO · DAY 76

🎚️ Sampler order — every prompt is sent with three numbers beside it, and the argument about them is always which VALUES to pick. This page is about something the argument assumes away: the values are applied in an order, two orders ship in mainstream stacks, and the same three numbers do not mean the same thing in both. Nothing here simulates language ability — twenty-four next-token situations are DATA, the settings grid is ENUMERATED, and every kept set is decided by running a real truncator, so each of the 26,208 cells is a count and never a sample. Exactly two numbers are declared, both on sliders and both labelled where they are used, and no panel marked parameter-free reads either. The parameter-free centrepiece needs no dial at all: send the same prompt with the same temperature and the same top_p to a stack that applies temperature first and to one that applies it last, and the surviving candidates differ on 43.39% of 10,296 cells — min_p is worse at 45.75% — while top_k, the knob everyone calls the crude one, differs on 0.00% of 8,424 cells with a worst-case distance of exactly 0. At the single commonest setting on the internet, temperature 0.7 with top_p 0.9, 9 of the 24 situations come out with a different candidate set depending on whose sampler you are on. 🔴 And the disagreement is not cosmetic: the emitted distribution moves by more than 0.10 total variation on 23.81% of nucleus cells and by more than 0.25 on 5.74%. 🔴 The repair I reached for died under measurement. Pin the pool with a top_k and keep the nucleus for shaping, and the immunity does NOT transfer: 26.57% of 92,664 three-knob cells still change with the temperature placement, and swapping the two truncators against each other changes the answer on a further 10.24% — only 66.52% of cells are immune to both. The reason is one line of every real implementation: each truncator sees the distribution RENORMALISED over the survivors of the one before it. 🔴 Which also kills the folk rule for stacking: setting top_k and top_p together does not apply whichever is tighter. On 10.31% of 2,376 pairs the composite is strictly tighter than both — up to 50.00% in the worst column — and it is looser than either on 0.00%. 🔴 top_p = 0.9 does not keep 90% of the mass and does not keep a tenth of anything: it retains 95.13% on average, overshoots by up to 10.00 points, and hands back between 1 and 11 candidates — exactly ONE on 33.33% of the corpus, which is greedy decoding wearing a creativity dial. Its mirror is just as unstable in the other axis: top_k = 1 retains anywhere from 14.83% to 99.99% of the mass, a spread of 85.16 points. 🔴 "Raise the temperature for more variety" is half false and the half is decidable: with temperature applied last the candidate pool is FROZEN — going from T = 1.0 to T = 2.0 adds exactly 0.00 candidates and reaches 0.9992 nats, against 5.88 candidates and 1.3196 nats — 1.32× the entropy — for the identical request on the other stack. 🔴 The declared half has a cliff rather than a slope: give each situation a tail of 2% shared by 4,000 unlisted tokens and one step of the temperature dial, 1.10 to 1.20, takes the nucleus from 4.88 candidates to 496.08 — a factor of 101.8 — while the other order sits at 4.13 and never moves. Two null results, located rather than claimed: at T = 1 the two stacks are the same computation and disagree on 0 of 264 cells, and top_k is order-proof on all 8,424 of its cells — the free fix, at zero tokens, is the knob you were told was obsolete. Three identities carry the page rather than three trends: two orders differ exactly when their kept sets differ, min_p is exactly a logit-margin cut of width T · ln(1/min_p) — agreeing with the closed form on 7,488 of 7,488 cells, which is why it is the order-sensitive one — and the nucleus can only shrink as temperature falls, checked at every adjacent pair with 0 violations. 261,332 in-page assertions plus 702,547 in an independent Node verifier that rebuilds the softmax in log space, the nucleus by binary search on a prefix-sum table, top-k by quickselect, min-p as a logit threshold, the tail by full expansion into four thousand individual tokens, and total variation from an explicit dense vector — and the verifier earned its keep, catching the engine rounding one extra tail token into the nucleus wherever the crossing landed exactly on a token boundary, which an absolute epsilon on a quotient in the thousands cannot see. Pure vanilla JavaScript, one file, no build step.

Two numbers on this page are declared about the world and everything else is a count. tail is the probability mass a situation's candidate list does not name — a real vocabulary holds a hundred thousand entries that individually carry nothing and together carry something — and tailN is how many tokens share it. They are the only two, they are both here, and the panels marked parameter-free never read them. Temperature, top_p, top_k and min_p are also on dials, but they are not assumptions — they are the subject: the whole page is about what a stack does with them, and the centrepiece enumerates every value on a grid rather than trusting the one under your cursor. A situation's logits are DATA. At T = 1 their softmax IS the declared distribution, and nothing anywhere is sampled: a rate is a count over a finite enumerated grid, so a 0.00% here is a zero and not "none seen".

Three numbers, and the order nobody prints

Every request you send carries a temperature and usually one or two truncation settings. The entire public conversation about them is about which values to choose: 0.7 for chat, 0 for extraction, top_p 0.9 because that is what the paper used. What that conversation assumes, without ever saying so, is that the three numbers are a specification — that they name a behaviour, and any correct implementation will produce it.

They are not a specification. They are three operations, and operations compose in an order. Two orders ship. The list of logits processors that generate builds applies temperature first and then the truncators. The default sampler chain in llama.cpp runs the truncators and applies temperature at the end. Both are defensible; neither is documented as a semantic choice; and they are not the same function.

Why the order can matter at all

Start from the one thing that is true in both stacks: the token is drawn from softmax(logits / T) restricted to whatever survived truncation. That means the final distribution is determined by two things — the temperature, and the surviving set — and the temperature is the same number in both stacks. So the two orders can differ only through the set. That is not a heuristic; it is an identity, and the page asserts it on every cell in both directions: whenever the sets are equal the distributions are bit-identical, and whenever they differ the distributions differ.

Now ask each truncator whether it notices the temperature.

Top-k takes the k highest. Temperature divides every logit by the same positive number, which cannot reorder them. So top-k picks the same tokens whichever side of it temperature sits on, and because the final softmax is the same either way, the emitted distributions are identical to the last bit. Eight thousand four hundred and twenty-four cells, zero disagreements, worst-case distance exactly zero. Top-k is the only order-proof knob on the page.

Top-p takes the shortest prefix whose mass reaches p. Temperature changes the mass. Sharpen the distribution and the prefix reaches p sooner, so fewer tokens survive; flatten it and more do. The kept set is a function of the temperature, so the placement of temperature decides what the set is. That is the 43.39%.

Min-p looks like it should be immune, because it is a ratio test: keep anything at least min_p times as likely as the best token. Ratios survive rescaling — but temperature is not a rescaling of probabilities, it is a rescaling of logits, and a ratio of probabilities is an exponentiated difference of logits. Work it through and min-p is exactly "keep every token within T · ln(1/min_p) of the top logit". The margin is proportional to T. Put temperature first and the margin scales with your temperature dial; put it last and the margin is frozen at ln(1/min_p). It is the most order-sensitive of the three, for a reason you can write on one line.

The renormalisation nobody mentions

There is a second order in the stack, and it is worse hidden. When two truncators run in sequence, the second one does not see the model's distribution. It sees the survivors of the first, renormalised to sum to one — because that is what a softmax over the remaining logits does, and every implementation works that way.

The consequence is that "set top_k and top_p, whichever is tighter wins" is false. Cut to the top eight and the mass of those eight is inflated to 1.0; a nucleus of 0.9 now crosses its threshold earlier than it would have on the full distribution, and keeps fewer tokens than either knob would alone. On this corpus that happens on 10.31% of the k×p pairs, reaching 50% in the tightest column, and it never goes the other way. The composite is a third setting, not the stricter of two.

It also means the truncators do not commute with each other. Swap top-k and top-p and the answer changes on 10.24% of the three-knob cells, with a worst case of half the probability mass moving. So when I reached for the obvious repair — use top-k to pin the pool and keep top-p for shaping, and inherit top-k's immunity — the measurement said no. Only two thirds of the cells are immune to both orderings. The immunity does not transfer, because the thing that made top-k immune was that it reads only the ranking, and the moment it runs beside a mass-based knob the mass is what decides.

What top_p 0.9 actually gives you

Two separate things are wrong with the folk description. The first is the direction of the guarantee: nucleus keeps the token that crosses the threshold, so the retained mass is at least p and never exactly p. Averaged over this corpus, top_p = 0.9 retains 95.13%, and on one situation it retains 100% — the overshoot reaches a full ten points.

The second is worse. A mass is not a count, and the count is what you experience. Across twenty-four situations, 0.9 hands back between one and eleven candidates. On a third of them it hands back exactly one, which means the creativity dial you set is greedy decoding. That is not a defect in nucleus sampling — it is nucleus sampling doing precisely what it says — but "0.9 keeps the top 10%" is a sentence that has never been true of anything.

The mirror holds for top-k, and the page prints it rather than hiding it, because top-k is the thing this page ends up recommending. Fixing the count means the mass floats: top_k = 1 retains anywhere from 14.83% to 99.99% of the distribution. Neither knob is stable. They are unstable on different axes, and only one of them is unstable in a way that also depends on whose code you are running.

The cliff at the top of the temperature dial

The last panel is the only one that reads a declared number, and it is the one that explains a thing everyone has seen. Give each situation a tail: two per cent of the mass, shared by four thousand tokens that individually carry nothing. Below a crossover the tail is invisible; every nucleus setting cuts it entirely. Then move the temperature dial by one step, 1.10 to 1.20, and the candidate pool goes from 4.88 tokens to 496.08. One step later it is 2,029.83.

That is not gradual degradation. Temperature above 1 lifts a hundred thousand tiny probabilities far more, in relative terms, than it lifts the handful of large ones, so the tail's collective mass grows until it is the majority of the distribution — and then nucleus, which was cutting it, has to include it to reach p. And on the other stack, where the nucleus ran at T = 1, the tail was cut before temperature ever touched it and the pool stays at 4.13 forever. Same request, same three numbers, two orders of magnitude apart.

So what should you do

The free fix is the knob you were told was obsolete. Top-k costs nothing, is order-proof across every cell on the page, and does not care which library is underneath. Its price is on the table and it is real: it is blind to the shape of the distribution, so the mass it keeps floats by eighty-five points across this corpus. If that price is unacceptable and you want the nucleus, then the number you set is not portable, and the honest thing is to record which stack it was tuned on next to the value — the same way you record a model version.

And one thing that costs nothing at all: if a setting has to behave identically everywhere, T = 1 is exactly where the two orders coincide, on all 264 cells, by construction. Which is a strange kind of advice — the setting that removes the ambiguity is the setting that does nothing.

This is not Day 67. That page branched the top-k first tokens to find a reasoning path and never changed a prompt; this one changes no decoding path and asks only what the truncators do to a distribution that is handed to them. It is not Day 3 either — nothing here is sampled, drawn or voted on. Every number is a count over an enumerated grid.

🔨 How it is built

Every number on the MEASURE tab comes from the block between ENGINE:BEGIN and ENGINE:END in this file's source. It touches no DOM, reads no clock and calls no random number generator. An independent Node verifier rebuilds each load-bearing structure a second way — the softmax in log space, the nucleus by binary search over a prefix-sum table, top-k by quickselect, min-p as a logit threshold, the tail by expanding it into individual tokens, and total variation from an explicit dense vector — and compares them cell for cell.

1. The corpus is data and the grid is enumerated

Twenty-four next-token situations across seven families — JSON, code, prose, enum, number, markup and a list — each one a list of candidate continuations with a LOGIT. Logits rather than probabilities, because temperature is an operation on logits; at T = 1 their softmax is the declared distribution. The shapes are spread deliberately: one candidate carrying 99.99%, an exact two-way tie, flat plateaus of five, geometric ladders of twenty-two. Thirty-nine temperatures, eleven nucleus settings, nine top-k values and eight min-p values, all enumerated, so every rate on the page is a count over a finite set.

2. A situation becomes groups, so the tail is exact

Every candidate is a group of one, sorted by logit descending. The declared tail is ONE group of four thousand identical members, whose logit is solved for from the declared MASS rather than the other way round. Because its members are indistinguishable, "how many of them does the nucleus keep" is a division and a ceiling rather than a loop, so a four-thousand token tail costs the same as a four-token one and the arithmetic stays exact. The verifier expands the tail into individual tokens and re-runs everything the slow way to prove the two agree.

3. The truncators are written the way real ones are

Top-k walks the sorted groups taking members until the budget runs out. Nucleus accumulates and keeps THROUGH the token that crosses p — that convention is the whole reason retained mass overshoots. Min-p thresholds at min_p times the top probability. All three honour min_tokens_to_keep = 1, and all three see the probabilities of the tokens that are still candidates, which is the renormalisation that Probe 3 is about.

4. A stack is a fold, and the identity falls out of it

A step is either a temperature or a truncator. A truncator drops the members it did not keep; a temperature changes the T that every later step and the final softmax will use. The final distribution is therefore always softmax(lg / T) over the survivors — which is why two orders can differ only through their kept set, and why that is an assertion on every cell rather than a claim in a paragraph.

5. Comparison is by set and by distance, separately

A kept set is compared by an exact key over group index and member count, so two runs agree only if they kept the same tokens in the same numbers. Distance is total variation, computed over the union of the two sets, and it handles a partially-kept tail by pairing the overlapping members and charging the difference for the rest. Both are reported, because "the sets differ" and "the answer differs enough to matter" are different questions and the page answers them separately: 43.39% against 23.81% over a tenth.

6. The claims are attacked rather than restated

The in-page assertions check that every distribution sums to one at every temperature on the grid; that the nucleus retains at least p and that dropping its last token would retain less than p, which is minimality and not an approximation; that top-k keeps exactly min(k, n); that min-p's kept set equals the closed-form logit margin on a sweep of the temperature grid, which the verifier then re-checks on all 7,488 of its cells; that the argmax survives every truncator in every order; that top-k's two orders are bit-identical; that equal sets imply equal distributions and unequal sets imply unequal ones; that the nucleus is monotone in temperature and the argmax probability is monotone the other way; that the composite of two truncators is a subset of both; that a frozen-pool stack really is frozen; that total variation is symmetric, zero on itself and bounded by one; and that the declared tail carries exactly the mass it was declared to carry at T = 1.

7. Both controls are printed, not just described

Top-k across every temperature and every k, and both orders at T = 1 across every nucleus setting. Both must be exactly zero. They are the difference between "the samplers disagree" and "my comparison is broken", and a page that reports the first without printing the second has not earned it.

8. Everything expensive is memoised, nothing is cached wrong

The parameter-free sweeps depend on the family selector and nothing else, so they are computed once per family and reused; the panels that read a dial are recomputed on every change, in a plain timer-driven pump so a slider drag never blocks the first paint. There is no animation frame, no clock and no random source anywhere in the file.
